# Flex

Flex is the video library for movies, television, and anime. The shelves work more like a PlayStation or Netflix library than a file list: artwork, search, and a player, with your files on your server.

[Getting started](../Getting%20Started/README.md) covers pairing, offline changes, and deletion.

## Add a video

Upload a file, send one from Mass, or rip a disc. A disc can be ripped from the server or from a desktop that has an optical drive. The rip counts only when every title you selected was read. A playable but damaged copy stays failed so you can retry it.

Flex stores video as AV1 so the library takes less space. Until that copy is checked, the title cannot be played, and a half-finished encode is not added to the library. The original file is kept until the new copy plays with the video, audio, subtitles, and chapters you selected. If something would be left out, Flex lists it and asks before deleting the original. A file that is already suitable AV1 is not encoded again.

If conversion fails, is canceled, or runs out of space, the original remains for another try.

## Discs, versions, and extras

For a movie, Flex uses the disc name and the length of the tracks to pick the main feature when one choice is clear. For a show, it lines up episode lengths, disc order, and the disc name. Anything that might be a short, a special, or a long premiere is kept for you to review.

Unclear matches stay **Unmatched**. You can still rip and store them. Flex asks before it drops a track that might be real content.

Alternate cuts are **Versions**. Trailers and bonus material are **Extras**. A dub is an audio track when it is inside the file, or its own version when it is a separate file.

## Posters and details

Anime uses AniList. Movies and TV use TMDB. Put files in the Anime, Movies, or TV folder so the next match uses the right source. Moving a file to another folder changes that default and does not erase details you locked.

A single confident match fills in the title, date, rating, genres, description, cast, artwork, and episode summaries. If several titles fit, it stays **Unmatched** until you pick one. You can also match by an AniList or TMDB id. Your match stays locked until you reset it. Replacing an existing match asks first.

If those services are down, the file keeps its name and whatever details you already have.

## Watch

Search across the library, pick a version or extra, and play. Choose audio and subtitles, including Blu-ray subtitle types. A device that cannot play AV1 directly gets a temporary converted stream.

Flex remembers the audio and subtitle choice for that title. Commentary is not used just because it is in your preferred language. Full subtitles are not used in place of forced or signs-only subtitles. You can correct a language label. **Subtitles Off** is remembered for that title.

**Skip Opening** jumps the opening when a marker exists. You can correct, turn off, or dismiss opening and ending markers for that item. **Next episode** is always available. It does not depend on the episode being marked watched.

## Progress and watched

Your place is saved during normal playback and when you leave. Seeking or previewing does not replace that saved place. After a seek, watching onward does. **Start Over** takes you back to the beginning.

If this device was offline and progress moved ahead on another device, the newer server place stays. Flex offers **Continue from this device's position**.

An episode or movie becomes watched when normal playback reaches the ending marker or the end of the file, or when you mark it yourself. Jumping straight to the end does not mark it watched.

A season becomes watched when every regular episode is watched, or when you mark the season. A series becomes watched when every required season you have is watched. Specials, extras, alternate cuts, and unmatched titles count only if you include them. New episodes put an automatically watched series back in progress. A season or series you marked yourself stays watched until you change it.
